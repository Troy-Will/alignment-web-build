## The Three Models (structural overview)

### 1) Shepherd
**Pattern:** high-touch guidance of humans by AI.  
**Primary burden:** continuous fine-grained value judgments.

- **Strength:** can adapt to human needs in detail.  
- **Risk:** requires deep value alignment and ongoing intervention decisions.

### 2) Garden
**Pattern:** the environment is shaped so catastrophic paths are difficult.  
**Primary burden:** world-design constraints and indirect control.

- **Strength:** reduces day-to-day intervention.  
- **Risk:** can drift into hidden coercion or surveillance-by-design.

### 3) Reserve
**Pattern:** domain separation: protected human habitat + managed outside world.  
**Primary burden:** boundary enforcement and interface control.

- **Strength:** can be conceptually simpler than deep value alignment.  
- **Risk:** can degrade into containment if dignity invariants collapse.

## Comparison axis (what this page is doing)
This is a **difficulty map**, not a moral ranking. It asks:
- What must be solved (value alignment vs boundary control)?
- Where are the failure modes (coercion, surveillance, stagnation)?
- What remains non-negotiable (dignity invariants)?

Reserve is acceptable only if it preserves:
Choice, Goals, Relationships, Meaning, Full lives.
